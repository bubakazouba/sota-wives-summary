Episode 1 · Oct 12 · 32 min

Marrying young, getting rejected by YC, and getting canceled

Sarah + Vivian, no guestYouTubeSpotify

The two hosts on marriage before the money, what getting canceled taught them, and what a YC partner actually thinks of the founders in her batch.

What they cover

  1. 1:00
    How they met. Sarah, then an MIT student and creator, cold-DM'd Vivian on LinkedIn and ended up interning at Vivian's startup, helping kick off growth for a new product. Sarah calls the cold DM "a very dark part of my history."
  2. 4:00
    Finding your husband before the exit. Sarah met Tyler while living under the stairs of a Hayes Valley startup house. His company was later acquired by Nvidia with big payouts for the small team. Dating before the money means "I know your intentions are clean." Vivian: "you either should be single or you should find someone that's like your ride or die."
  3. 6:00
    How do you know? Vivian: "You just have to bet on the person." Her husband once planned to move to Singapore for a sovereign wealth fund job. She told him good luck; he stayed and went into venture.
  4. 7:00
    The China detour. A week before the wedding, Sarah told Tyler she was set on a one-year master's in China to escape the tech bubble. He came around. Sarah has never lived alone and once had four-plus male roommates in SF.
  5. 8:48
    Did marriage cost them ambition? Sarah says it changed her priorities: she no longer wants to give her whole life to founding a company, but she is still career-driven and looks up to her female managers with kids at Cerebras. Both say a stable partner lets you take more risk.
  6. 10:50
    Vivian's cancellation. Months before SB 1047 went public, she was attacked in a cafe by a man with a metal bar and needed surgery; she says he was out on house arrest about two days later. Her tweet that "it's easier to be a homeless guy in California than it is to be an AI developer" went viral with critics. Lesson: only get canceled for something you actually believe.
  7. 15:30
    Sarah's "hot girl walk." A photo of an MIT reunion walk through the Marina drew millions of views and cruel comments. She took it down only because the other women in it were getting hate.
  8. 18:04
    "Psychological violence." Sarah's 25th-birthday recap (growing online, getting married, the Cerebras IPO) went viral weeks later when someone called it psychological violence. Vivian pushed back, and the post came down.
  9. 19:30
    The name. Sarah pitched "Trophy Wives" twice; Vivian left it on read both times.
  10. 20:47
    What a YC partner thinks of her batch. Vivian says you can tell early who has "the right bones." Warning signs: founders groomed by elite schools to follow instructions and collect badges, not talking to users, avoiding discomfort, quitting after one "no." Some teams that look doomed mid-batch come back strong months later.
  11. 23:47
    Thrift House. Sarah co-founded a startup with her then-boyfriend as an MIT sophomore. They went through Z Fellows, got rejected by YC, then got into a new accelerator whose roughly $625K required dropping out. They went back to school, "and that killed the startup basically." Vivian thinks it was a mistake that YC passed.
  12. 28:54
    SF lore. Sarah's first SF party was a cat party where nobody spoke, only meowed. Vivian's post-college apartment kept adding roommates until five people and three startups shared it.

Lines worth keeping

"If everybody in your life who you respect and look up to is telling you not to do something and then you do it anyway and you are successful, that usually means you are quite a good startup founder."Vivian · ~25:00
"You have to get canceled for something you actually believe in."Vivian · ~15:00
"It's much easier to go and rip someone apart on Twitter than to go to therapy yourself."Vivian · ~19:00

Takeaways

  • Marrying young, before any success, is framed as trust and stability that makes risk easier, even though it reshaped what "ambitious" means.
  • Online hate says more about the hater. Stand by what you post and keep posting.
  • Vivian's founder filter: agency, talking to users, tolerating rejection, and enough conviction to ignore advice.
Episode 2 · Nov 5 · 40 min

Shutting down a startup, joining an AI lab, and leaving it

Guest: Thariq, the "third SOTA wife"YouTubeSpotify

Recorded the day after Thariq's last day at Anthropic. He talks through over-raising, shutting down his company, why he joined a lab, and why he thinks the labs are over-idolized.

What they cover

  1. 1:01
    Outed on Twitter. Thariq deactivated his work email around 3 PM on his last day; by 6 PM it was on Twitter and CEOs were in his DMs, so he decided to just write it out. The trio jokes they cover all of YC: partner, rejected, accepted.
  2. 3:02
    The COVID batch. YC Winter 2020 started in a Mountain View warehouse. Demo Day turned into a single slide investors could thumbs-up. His batch raised around $2M seeds, roughly double what older alumni remembered.
  3. 5:17
    Over-raising Multiverse. His company, an online platform for games like Dungeons & Dragons, raised a big Series A on COVID gaming hype and sat on about $15M with seven or eight people, well ahead of traction. After five years he gave up and returned the remaining money.
  4. 8:58
    Toronto, Bay Area high schools, kids. Bay Area high-school alumni bond over shared "trauma." The hosts debate South Bay schools, Harker's college-level tuition, and homeschooling. Thariq wants his future kids to be happy "airheads" who don't over-introspect.
  5. 12:39
    Tech Twitter, deflated. He once thought Twitter was "Renaissance Florence" and now finds it "very overhyped." Parties made of Twitter people are "the worst." His rule: never meet anon accounts.
  6. 18:32
    Going viral with Claude Code. He believes he was the first to make videos with Claude Code. If AI does something "kind of good" today, it will be really good in 6–12 months. Good content needs real, boring work underneath, and people who get famous often stop doing it.
  7. 20:26
    Why Anthropic, and the idolization problem. From Sonnet 3.5 on, Anthropic was clearly best at coding, and Opus 4 sealed it for him. He now calls the labs "huge talent black holes," like early Google but amplified. "Everyone at the lab is just a person."
  8. 22:33
    Can startups survive the labs? Vivian: every model release triggers "mass murder of all these startups" talk, yet Anthropic itself once looked hopeless next to OpenAI and Google. Startups are a momentum game, and overly rational people talk themselves into joining a lab instead.
  9. 27:50
    Don't make a company your identity. Labs differ by founders and business model; Anthropic is "more of a coding enterprise company." Every institution, including MIT, YC and Anthropic, has LARPing. What matters is who you work with and where you have impact.
  10. 29:53
    Sorting the labs into Hogwarts houses. Thariq: Anthropic is Hufflepuff, "competent, earnest people"; OpenAI maybe Gryffindor, heroic with some brashness. Sarah is a self-reported Slytherin.
  11. 31:50
    Friendly rivals. Sarah and Tyler worked at competing companies. Thariq compares lab rivalry to sports teams: play hard, but you can be traded any time. The worst outcome for AI companies is any one of them to stop growing.
  12. 35:21
    Honeypots and the apocalypse. On reports of anti-honeypot training at OpenAI: "I just write TypeScript. I'm like so not worth honeypotting." Vivian would rather die than survive a Walking Dead world; Thariq says there'd be "no Twitter, there's no TypeScript."

Lines worth keeping

"Spiritually it's bad to raise too much money, I think, because then you feel like you've done something but you haven't."Thariq · ~8:00
"Courage is in shorter supply than genius."Thariq · ~24:00
"If your job is to make interesting content, you have to do interesting work that's kind of boring."Thariq · ~20:00
"I think it's healthy to not tie your identity to one company or one institution."Thariq · ~28:00

Takeaways

  • Money ahead of traction feels like progress and isn't; it also makes a good exit harder.
  • The labs are over-idolized. Startups still win with momentum, simple ideas executed hard, and courage over overthinking.
  • Lab rivalry is closer to sports than war: a positive-sum market with lots of cross-lab friendship.
Across both episodes

Recurring threads

  • YC as the shared axis. A partner, a reject and an alum, all circling what YC actually rewards.
  • Badges vs. conviction. Elite-school badge collecting and "LARPing" startups, against founders who ignore advice and bet on themselves.
  • Tech Twitter as stage and target. Cancellations in EP1, getting outed and going viral in EP2.
  • Work and life blur in SF. Startup houses, couples at rival companies, friends who are also your network.
  • Just doing vs. introspecting. "Bet on the person," don't overthink your way out of a startup, raise kids who don't over-introspect.
